How Value Score Works
Employment outcomes per dollar spent

The Value Score measures how much bar-required employment outcome a school delivers relative to what it costs. It's calculated as:

Value Score = (Bar-Required Employment %) ÷ (Net Tuition / $10,000)

Net tuition is resident tuition minus the school's median grant amount. A school charging $20,000 net with an 85% bar-required employment rate scores much higher than a school charging $60,000 for the same outcome.

This ranking intentionally excludes T14 schools, which operate in a different market where prestige, BigLaw access, and network effects justify their price premiums in ways that a cost-efficiency metric doesn't capture. For most students weighing six-figure debt, comparing Tier 2 and Tier 3 schools on a value basis is the more relevant analysis.

Net tuition is an estimate. It uses the school's median grant award from ABA 509 data as the offset. Your actual scholarship will depend on your LSAT, GPA, and application strategy. Use the Scholarship Estimator and Debt Planner to model your specific scenario.
